Getting started

Benday reads from a server you run yourself, on a computer, NAS or home server: Komga, Kavita, Stump, Suwayomi, BookOrbit, Audiobookshelf (its ebooks and comics), or any server with an OPDS catalog, such as Codex, calibre-web, calibre's own content server, LANraragi, Mylar3, Ubooquity, COPS or Jellyfin's OPDS plugin. Benday doesn't come with any comics: it shows what's on your server.

  1. Have a server. Your own, or one a friend shares with you. New to this? Komga is a good start for comics and manga: follow its installation guide and add a library. Supported servers lists what works where.
  2. Install Benday from Google Play on your Android phone or tablet (Android 7 or newer).
  3. Sign in with the server's address, or the catalog's OPDS address, and your account. See Signing in.
  4. Start reading. Your libraries appear under Libraries (on an OPDS catalog, the catalog itself); anything you've started shows on Home.
  5. Take the tour if you like. On the sign-in screen, New here? Take a quick tour walks through the form; after your first sign-in Benday offers a tour of the app, which stays under ⋮ › Take the tour.

Supported servers

Benday speaks Komga, Kavita, Stump, Suwayomi, BookOrbit and Audiobookshelf natively, through their own APIs, and reads from OPDS catalogs, the common feed format many servers offer. With Komga you get everything Benday does; with Kavita nearly all of it; with Stump the library, smart lists, progress and new books as they're scanned; with Suwayomi your manga library, its categories, progress and new chapters; with BookOrbit the library, its collections and smart scopes, progress and live updates; with Audiobookshelf the ebooks and comics of your book libraries, collections, progress and live updates; OPDS catalogs give you browsing, reading and downloads, and how much reaches the server depends on the server. Without any of these, Benday reads from a plain folder on a WebDAV server such as Nextcloud, Seafile or a NAS.

Formats

  • CBZ and ZIP archives of images.
  • CBR and RAR archives, except RAR5 ones: convert those to CBZ.
  • PDF, page by page.
  • EPUB, comics and novels, and KEPUB (Kobo's EPUB), read as EPUB.
  • DjVu, from BookOrbit 3.5 or newer, which renders its pages.

Files on your device can be CBZ, CBR, PDF or EPUB (without DRM).

Signing in

The sign-in screen takes a server address or an OPDS catalog address, and finds out by itself what kind of server it is.

Komga

Enter the address you use to open Komga in a browser, for example http://192.168.1.20:25600 on your home network or https://comics.example.com from anywhere. If Komga runs under a path (https://example.com/komga), include it.

Sign in with your Komga email and password, or switch the sign-in form to API key and paste a key instead (Komga 1.15 or newer). Create one in Komga under Account settings, or once signed in under Settings › API keys. An API key can be revoked on its own without changing your password, and it works for accounts that sign in through single sign-on.

Kavita

Enter Kavita's address, the one you open in a browser (http://192.168.1.20:5000, for example), and sign in with your Kavita username and password, or choose API key and paste the key from your Kavita account settings. With a password, Benday uses it once to fetch your account's API key and keeps only the key. Pasting your Kavita OPDS address (…/api/opds/<key>) works too. Full Kavita mode needs Kavita 0.8.9 or newer; an older Kavita works as an OPDS catalog.

Stump

Enter Stump's address, the one you open in a browser (http://192.168.1.20:10801, for example), and sign in with your Stump username and password, or choose API key and paste a Stump API key. With a password, Benday uses it once to create an API key for this device, listed as "Benday (…)" in Stump's settings, and keeps only the key. Pasting your Stump OPDS address works too. Full Stump mode needs Stump 0.1.8 or newer.

The account needs Stump's API keys permission, or must be the server owner; new Stump users have no permissions until the owner grants them. Without it, Benday says so, and Stump's OPDS catalog still works with the same username and password. An older Stump works as an OPDS catalog.

Suwayomi

Enter Suwayomi's address, the one you open in a browser (http://192.168.1.20:4567, for example), and sign in with the username and password of Suwayomi's login, or choose None if your Suwayomi asks for none. Benday works with each of Suwayomi's login modes (basic authentication, the login page and the web UI's login) and keeps the password to sign in again when Suwayomi's short-lived sign-ins run out. Pasting your Suwayomi OPDS address works too. Full Suwayomi mode needs Suwayomi 2.1 or newer; an older one works as an OPDS catalog.

Benday shows the manga in your Suwayomi library, with its categories as collections; it never browses sources or installs extensions. Chapters download page by page, whether Suwayomi downloaded them or not.

BookOrbit

Enter BookOrbit's address, the one you open in a browser (http://192.168.1.20:3000, for example), and sign in with your BookOrbit username and password: your account, not an OPDS login made in BookOrbit's settings. Benday uses the password once and keeps only the sign-in BookOrbit gives this device ("Benday (…)" among your sessions in BookOrbit's settings), which it renews by itself, also once a day in the background so a week without Benday doesn't end it. Full BookOrbit mode needs BookOrbit 3.4 or newer; an older one, or a BookOrbit that allows only single sign-on, works as an OPDS catalog.

Benday shows your BookOrbit libraries, with series and books without a series side by side, and BookOrbit's collections and smart scopes as read lists. Comics open in the reading direction set in BookOrbit. Downloading a whole book needs BookOrbit's download permission; without it comics download page by page, and PDFs and EPUBs are read online.

Catalogs in the newer OPDS 2 format work too: paste their address (…/opds/v2/catalog on Komga, …/opds/v2.0/ on Codex, …/opds/v2.0/catalog on Stump). You browse, search and filter them as any catalog, and books are downloaded to read: Benday doesn't stream their pages yet. Progress reaches the server where the catalog offers OPDS Progression (Codex and Stump do). Books a library lends with copy protection (DRM) aren't offered. For Komga, Codex and Stump the 1.2 address above remains the better choice: pages stream, and Benday knows each server's extras there.

A folder on a WebDAV server

No library server? Benday also reads comics and books straight from a folder on a WebDAV server: Nextcloud's or ownCloud's Files, Seafile, a NAS (Synology, QNAP), or rclone serve webdav. On the sign-in screen, turn on Folder on a WebDAV server, enter the folder's WebDAV address and sign in with your account (on Nextcloud, an app password if you use two-factor sign-in).

Benday lists CBZ, CBR (ZIP and RAR), PDF and EPUB files. If the folder's own folders hold folders of books (Comics/, Books/), each is a library; every folder with books in it is a series, its files in name order. A cover.jpg or folder.jpg in a folder is its cover, a picture with a book's name that book's. Books open once they are downloaded (comics and PDFs in the background, EPUBs as a download); progress is kept on the device, and travels between your devices with Sync between devices. Search finds books and folders by name; Home shows Continue reading and the newest files. A server that asks for Digest sign-in works too, but shows no covers yet.

HTTPS and self-signed certificates

To read away from home, the server must be reachable from outside: through a reverse proxy with HTTPS, or a VPN such as WireGuard or Tailscale. Downloaded books work without any connection.

Servers with a normal certificate (Let's Encrypt, for example) just work. If your server uses a self-signed certificate, Benday shows the certificate's details and fingerprint and asks whether to trust it. Only trust it if it's your server: Benday then remembers that certificate for that address and warns you if it ever changes.

Home and away addresses

A server you reach at one address at home (http://192.168.1.20:25600) and another one elsewhere (https://comics.example.com, or a Tailscale name) can keep both. In Settings › Servers, edit the server and fill in Away address. Benday then picks the one that answers: on Wi-Fi it tries the home address first, on mobile data the away one, and if the first doesn't answer it uses the other within a fraction of a second. When the network changes, or you come back to the app, it checks again and switches by itself. The server's row shows Home or Away for the address in use.

It is the same server and account either way: downloads, reading progress, lists and bookmarks stay as they are. Each address keeps its own http or https, and a self-signed certificate is trusted per address.

Several servers

Settings › Servers keeps more than one server, of any kind; switch between them there. Tracker links are kept per server.

KOReader sync

Calibre-Web-Automated, Calibre-Web NextGen, Grimmory, BookLore and BookOrbit don't take reading progress over OPDS, but they all run a KOReader sync server, the one the KOReader e-reader app uses. Benday sends your position there, so the server's own reader and KOReader see it, and brings back the books you finished there.

  • Switch it on on the server. On Calibre-Web-Automated and Calibre-Web NextGen the admin turns KOReader sync on for the server. On Grimmory, BookLore and BookOrbit each user turns it on in their KOReader settings, and sets a KOReader username and password there.
  • The login. Benday tries the catalog's username and password first. If your KOReader login on Grimmory, BookLore or BookOrbit is a different one, open Manage servers (Settings › Servers), edit the catalog and choose KOReader sync: enter that login and press Test. Leave it empty to use the catalog's. Calibre-Web-Automated and Calibre-Web NextGen always use the catalog's login.
  • Downloaded books only. KOReader sync recognises a book by a fingerprint of the server's file, not by its title, so Benday needs the downloaded file to match it. These servers don't stream pages, so their books are downloaded before they open anyway; if you remove a download, that book's progress stays on the device until you download it again.

If the server turns the login away or sync is off, Benday says so on the book's sheet and keeps your progress on the device.

A KOReader sync server of your own

For any other catalog without a progress sync of its own (calibre-web, or a plain OPDS catalog), Benday can use a separate KOReader sync server, such as the one your KOReader devices already use (koreader-sync-server, kosync-dotnet). In Manage servers, edit the catalog and choose KOReader sync: enter the sync server's address and your username and password there, press Test, then Save. As above, it works for books downloaded whole, and books you finished on another device show as read.

Home and library

Home is a column of shelves: Continue reading, On deck (the next book of series you're reading), new books, Recently added, Reading lists and Want to read (Komga and Kavita), plus Upcoming (the next book of series still running, with an estimated date) and Discover on Komga. On servers with read lists, Reading order at the top is the read list you last opened a book from (a reading order such as a crossover event), scrolled to where you left off: the furthest book in the list you've started or finished, here or anywhere else, or the next one once it's finished (earlier unread books are skipped), earlier and later ones a swipe away. A read list's Read button picks the same book. Once you've finished a list's last book it leaves the shelf (another list you've been reading takes its place), and it comes back, at the first new book, when books are added to its end on the server. Reorder or hide them in Settings › Home shelves. To add your own, filter a library and choose Save as Home shelf.

Updates (See all on the New books shelf) lists new books by day and series and marks the ones added since you last looked; the filter button narrows it to series you're reading.

Libraries, Collections and Read lists mirror your Komga, Kavita, Stump, Suwayomi, BookOrbit or Audiobookshelf server (on Stump, its smart lists are the read lists; on Suwayomi, its categories are the collections; on BookOrbit, its collections and smart scopes are the read lists), with search, filters (read status, genre, publisher, age rating and more) and sorting. On Kavita, a series' books are grouped under headers: each volume, then chapters outside a volume, then specials. Tap a header to fold it away, or use its menu to mark the whole volume read or unread. On tablets, lists and details sit side by side.

On an OPDS catalog, Benday shows the catalog as the server arranges it, with its search and filters where it has them. Pin any page of the catalog to Home; Continue reading sits above the pins.

Long-press a book or series for quick actions: mark read or unread, download or open the series, and on Komga add to a read list (or a collection, for admins).

Benday uses the series' reading direction from the server (Komga's reading direction, Kavita's reading profile, the Stump library's reading settings), and guesses webtoon from very tall pages. Change it from the reader's toolbar for this series only, or set a default in Settings › Reader.

Turning pages

Tap the left or right edge, swipe, or use the volume keys (Settings › Reader › Tap zones and volume keys). Keyboards and Bluetooth page turners work too: arrow keys, Page Up/Down and space. With a Samsung S Pen, press the button for the next page, double-press for the previous one, or swipe the pen left or right (Air actions; remap them in Android Settings › Advanced features › S Pen). Tap the middle of the page to show the toolbar and the page slider.

Spreads and wide pages

In landscape, and on tablets, Benday can show two pages side by side. If a spread starts on the wrong page, shift it by one. In portrait, wide double pages can be split into two pages or turned sideways.

Guided view

Guided view moves panel by panel, which suits phones. Turn it on from the reader's toolbar.

Bookmarks and page actions

Bookmark a page from the toolbar; all bookmarks of a book are listed in the bookmarks sheet. Long-press a page to save or share it, or (as a Komga admin) make it the book's or series' cover.

EPUB books

Novels and other EPUBs read in one of two ways, chosen at the bottom of the Aa sheet. Scroll: each chapter is one long page; keep pulling past its end and the next chapter opens, pull down at its top for the end of the previous one. Pages: swipe sideways or tap the left or right edge to turn a page (from the right in right-to-left books), two columns side by side on a tablet held sideways; the bottom bar counts the chapter's pages.

The Aa button opens the text settings: text size, line spacing, side margins, font, alignment and the page's colours (auto, light, sepia or dark). They apply to every EPUB you read on this device, streamed or downloaded.

Japanese, Chinese and Korean books set in vertical lines read vertically: lines run down the page and on to the left, a chapter scrolls to the left, and pages turn from right to left (swipe right or tap the left edge to go on). For such books the Aa sheet also has Writing direction: Auto, Horizontal or Vertical, kept for each book.

  • Font: the book's own (Publisher's font, where you start), the system's serif or sans, or one of the fonts that come with Benday: Literata, Atkinson Hyperlegible (made for low vision), OpenDyslexic and a monospace font.
  • Alignment: Original keeps the book's, Left sets ragged lines, Justified lines up both edges and hyphenates words in the book's language.
  • Highlights and notes: select text and choose Highlight to mark it in the colour you used last (yellow at first), or Note to mark it and write a note; Copy and Share work on the selected text. Tap a highlight to change its colour (yellow, green, blue or pink), add or edit its note, or delete it. The Highlights tab next to Contents lists the book's highlights in reading order: tap one to jump to it, long-press it for its colours and note, and Share sends them all as Markdown. Highlights are kept on the device and follow you to your other devices like bookmarks: through your Komga account, or on other servers through Sync between devices. A highlight whose text isn't in the book any more (another edition) is listed as not found rather than moved.
  • Look up and Wikipedia: select a word and choose Look up for its definitions in a sheet over the page, or Wikipedia for the article's summary; Read the article opens the whole article in the sheet, and links stay on Wikipedia. Words are looked up in the book's language (English when the book doesn't say): first in your own dictionaries, then on Wiktionary, whose definitions are in English. Nothing leaves the device until you tap, and answers are kept for 30 days. For lookups without a connection, open Settings › Dictionaries and download one of Benday's dictionaries: Wiktionary's for English, German, French, Spanish and Swedish (definitions in their own language), Swedish words with English definitions, and Lexin's Swedish dictionary. The list and the files come from GitHub, nothing about you is sent, and Update appears when a newer version is out; tap a dictionary for its source and licence. Downloads follow Download only on Wi-Fi: without Wi-Fi, Benday asks first. You can also import a StarDict dictionary of your own (the .ifo, .idx and .dict or .dict.dz files, or a .zip of them), and tap it to choose the language its words are in.
  • Search in book (the magnifier) finds a word or phrase in every chapter and lists the matches with the text around them, chapter by chapter, as they come. Tap one to jump there; the match is marked on the page. A streamed book's chapters are fetched from the server once, so the first search takes longest; a downloaded book is searched on the device, also offline.

Display settings

The Reader settings button (the sliders) in the reader's toolbar opens the display settings: page fit, zoom, crop borders, background, brightness, contrast, saturation, colour filters, sharpening, and in webtoon mode side padding, gaps and auto-scroll speed. Choose whether changes apply to this series only or to every series; on Komga, per-series settings follow you to your other devices.

Settings › Look changes the whole app's look: eight styles, each with a light and dark side, following the system's dark mode or set by hand. On an e-ink screen, turn on Settings › E-ink mode: black on white, no animations and instant page turns.

Downloads and offline

  • Download a book, a whole series, a collection or a read list from its menu (on an OPDS catalog, from the book's sheet). Downloads continue in the background, with a progress notification.
  • Smart downloads (Downloads › Queue & settings) keeps the next one to five books of the series you're reading downloaded, so the next one is always there. Delete after reading removes a book once you've finished it.
  • Auto-download new books of a series when they arrive on a Komga server.
  • Download only on Wi-Fi and a storage limit (Settings › Downloads). Over the limit, the oldest books you've finished are removed; unread books never are.
  • Simultaneous downloads: up to three books download at once (two by default).
  • SD card: on a device with a memory card, Downloads › Queue & settings › Download location keeps downloads on the card, and offers to move the ones you already have.
  • Downloads › Library shows everything on the device, grouped by series, even with no connection.

Reading progress made offline is saved on the device and sent to the server the next time you're connected, on every server that takes progress (see Supported servers).

New-book notifications

Turn on Settings › Notifications › New books to get a notification when a series you follow or read gets a new book. Benday asks for Android's permission to show notifications; if you refuse, the switch stays off and Benday offers Android's settings, where you can allow them later.

  • Which series: the ones you auto-download (Komga) and the ones you turn on with Notify about new books in a series' menu (on Kavita, Stump, Suwayomi and BookOrbit, that is how you choose them). Turn on Also series I'm reading to include every series with a book read or started.
  • Which servers: every saved Komga, Kavita, Stump, Suwayomi and BookOrbit server, signed in with what is saved (never a new login), at its home or away address, whichever answers. OPDS catalogs aren't checked.
  • When: every 3, 6 (the default), 12 or 24 hours in the background, when Android lets it run (with enough battery, and only on Wi-Fi if you choose); also when the app starts, and while it is open, soon after the server reports new books. A check asks each server one small question when nothing is new.
  • One notification per series, grouped when several series have new books. Tap it to open the series, on the server it came from.
  • Books you've already read or started, and books you've already seen in Updates, aren't announced. The first check only notes what is there, so turning it on doesn't announce your whole library.

Sync between devices

On Komga, Benday keeps its shared data in your Komga account, so there is nothing extra to set up:

  • Reading progress (Komga's own, also seen by Komga's web reader and other apps)
  • Bookmarks, ratings and Want to read
  • Per-series display settings and reading direction

The app's theme, look, image quality and smart downloads setting follow your Komga account too.

On Kavita, reading progress is Kavita's own, so Kavita's web reader and your other devices see it; bookmarks, ratings and display settings can follow you through WebDAV (below). The same goes for Stump: progress is Stump's own, which its web reader sees. And for Suwayomi: progress is Suwayomi's own, which its web UI sees. And for BookOrbit: progress is BookOrbit's own, which its web reader and KOReader devices see; progress KOReader or a Kobo sends there shows up in Benday as it happens. On an OPDS catalog, progress reaches the server where Benday knows how (the table above, and KOReader sync); otherwise it stays on the device.

Bookmarks, ratings and more on the other servers: WebDAV

On Kavita, Stump, Suwayomi, BookOrbit and OPDS servers, Benday can keep your data the same on all your devices through a folder on a WebDAV server of your own: Nextcloud, ownCloud, a NAS or any WebDAV share. In Settings › Sync between devices, enter the WebDAV address, your username and password (on Nextcloud, an app password) and a folder, tap Test, then Turn on; set up your other devices with the same folder. Benday syncs when you open the app, soon after a change, on Sync now, and every six hours in the background while the app is closed (when the battery isn't low). The files go only to your WebDAV server; Komga's data keeps syncing through your Komga account.

Tested with Nextcloud, ownCloud (10 and Infinite Scale), Apache, nginx and rclone. On Nextcloud, make the app password under Personal settings › Security › Devices & sessions (Create new app password) and use the address https://your-server/remote.php/webdav; if you enter the server's own address, Test offers the right one. Infinite Scale needs basic auth switched on by its admin (PROXY_ENABLE_BASIC_AUTH). Servers that ask for Digest sign-in (Apache's mod_auth_digest, some NAS boxes) work as well. Behind nginx, raise client_max_body_size if Benday says the server refused a file as too large.

Under What syncs, each kind of data has its own switch:

  • Bookmarks, Ratings, Want to Read (on Stump, Suwayomi and BookOrbit a list of Benday's own; Kavita keeps its own list) and Series reader settings, per server.
  • OPDS reading progress and OPDS Home shelves: a book read on one device shows up in Continue reading on the other.
  • Look and reader defaults: theme, colours, image quality and the reader's default display settings. While it is on, the look no longer comes from your Komga account.
  • Saved servers: names, addresses and usernames, never passwords or keys. Your other devices list them in Manage servers under From your other devices; tap Add and enter the password.
  • Reading history and stats: off until you switch it on. Each device's reading is added up on the others.

A server one device saved with its home address as the main one and another with its away address is matched up through Saved servers (keep that switch on): its bookmarks, ratings and the rest follow you either way. On OPDS catalogs, ratings and settings of a feed are matched once the device has opened that feed.

Downloads stay on each device.

Encrypting the sync folder

If someone else runs your WebDAV server (a hosted Nextcloud, a storage box), switch on Encrypt the sync folder and choose a passphrase of at least 12 characters (a few unrelated words work well). Benday then encrypts every file in the folder on the device (AES-256-GCM, with a key made from the passphrase by Argon2id), so the server sees only file names and sizes. Making the key takes a few seconds on a phone. The passphrase itself isn't stored; the key stays in Android's encrypted storage on the device and is never in backups.

Your other devices stop syncing and say the folder is encrypted until you tap Enter passphrase there and type the same one. Older Benday versions stop too and ask to be updated, so nothing is written to the folder unencrypted. Switch it off on any device that has the passphrase to store the folder unencrypted again; your other devices follow. If the folder becomes unencrypted any other way (its settings file changed or deleted on the server), Benday stops syncing and asks: Keep it unencrypted or Encrypt it again (with a new passphrase). After turning encryption on again, check on every device that Encrypt the sync folder shows as on.

A forgotten passphrase can't be recovered. Nothing is lost, though: each device keeps its own data. Tap Start the folder over on one of them and choose a new passphrase: it deletes Benday's files in the folder (nothing else you keep there) and uploads that device's data again, encrypted with the new one; enter it on your other devices. Start over without encryption is there too. If one of your devices has been retired, data that only it had synced may not come back after a start over or after two devices turned encryption on at the same moment; devices still in use upload theirs again. Nextcloud, ownCloud and some storage boxes keep older versions and deleted files: delete the copies from before encryption there if they shouldn't stay readable.

Ratings and Discover

Discover is for Komga. Rate a series from its page. Ratings, together with what you've read, feed the Discover shelf, which suggests unread series that share writers, artists, genres or tags with the ones you liked, and says why. Want to read is a list of series to start later, on Komga, Kavita, Stump, Suwayomi and BookOrbit: on Kavita it is Kavita's own list, the one its web reader shows.

Tracking and Komf

MangaUpdates: sign in under Settings › Tracking, link a series from its menu (Track on…), and Benday updates your MangaUpdates list as you read. It works on Komga. If MangaUpdates is further along than your server, Benday offers to mark the earlier books read.

Komf is a metadata tool for Komga. If you run one and are a Komga admin, enter its address under Settings › Metadata › Komf; Identify with Komf and Auto-match with Komf then appear in a series' menu.

Files on your device

The Local files tab opens CBZ, CBR and PDF files (and ZIP or RAR archives of images) and EPUBs from your device without a server. EPUBs open in the book reader, with search, highlights and notes, look-up and text settings; copy-protected (DRM) EPUBs can't be opened. Benday remembers where you stopped in each.

Privacy and app lock

  • App lock asks for your fingerprint, face or screen lock when Benday opens. It needs a screen lock on the device.
  • Incognito reads without recording progress or history, until you turn it off.
  • Benday sends nothing to its developer. See the privacy policy.

Backup and restore

Settings › Backup & restore saves a file with your settings, bookmarks, reading history and statistics, per-series reading modes, and server addresses and usernames. Passwords aren't included, so after restoring on a new phone you sign in again (an OPDS address with an API key in it is saved as it is). Downloads aren't included.

Troubleshooting

"Can't reach the server"

Open the same address in the phone's browser. If that fails too, the phone can't reach the server: check that you're on the same network (or the VPN is on), the port is right, and there's no firewall in the way. localhost means the phone itself, so use the server's network address instead.

On Android 17, Benday needs your permission to reach anything on your home network. Android asks whether Benday may "find, connect to, and determine the relative position of nearby devices": that is Android's name for it, and Benday uses it only to reach your servers. If you refused, the sign-in screen says so and offers Allow; you can also allow Nearby devices for Benday in Android's settings. Servers reached over the internet or a VPN don't need it.

"This isn't a server Benday supports or an OPDS catalog"

The address answered, but not as Komga, Kavita, Stump, Suwayomi, BookOrbit or an OPDS catalog. A login page (an SSO proxy, for example) or a wrong path is in the way. For Komga, Kavita, Stump, Suwayomi and BookOrbit, use the address that opens the server itself; for other servers, the catalog's address (see Signing in).

"This is Grimmory's Komga-compatible API"

Grimmory and BookLore offer a read-only imitation of Komga's API, which can't save progress. Add their OPDS catalog instead (…/api/v1/opds) with the same username and password.

Certificate errors

Check the certificate is valid for the name you typed. For a self-signed certificate, accept the trust dialog when Benday offers it. If the certificate changed (renewed), Benday asks again.
